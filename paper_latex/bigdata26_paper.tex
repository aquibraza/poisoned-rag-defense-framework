\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}
% Budget: ~0.75 pg. Six short blocks; no block should exceed one paragraph.

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) answers a query in two stages: a retriever
selects a small set of passages from a corpus, and a generator conditions on them
when producing its answer~\cite{lewis2020rag}. Retrieval is typically dense,
embedding query and passages in a shared space and returning the top-$k$ passages
by inner product or cosine similarity~\cite{karpukhin2020dpr,izacard2022contriever}.
What makes this attractive --- answers follow from editing the corpus rather than
the model --- is also what creates the attack surface studied here. Corpora are
often large, externally sourced, or only semi-trusted, so a party able to write
into one may influence generated answers without access to model weights, the
prompt template, or the retriever, provided the inserted content is retrieved.
Retrieved context is an untrusted input channel, and corpus contents are part of
the trust boundary.

\subsection{Knowledge Poisoning and PoisonedRAG}
Knowledge poisoning targets that channel: the attacker inserts or rewrites a small
number of passages so that retrieval brings poisoned evidence into the context of
a target query and the generator produces a chosen incorrect answer. PoisonedRAG
states the two conditions a poisoned passage must meet --- a \emph{retrieval}
condition, remaining similar enough to the target query to enter the top-$k$, and
a \emph{generation} condition, supporting the target answer strongly enough for
the generator to adopt it --- under a poison budget of $N$ authored passages per
target query that is small relative to the corpus, and in a black-box variant
requiring no gradient access~\cite{zou2025poisonedrag}. Related work optimizes
poison directly against dense retrievers by discrete token search or in
continuous embedding space~\cite{su2025aggd,li2025continuous}.
We do not propose a stronger attack: PoisonedRAG-style poison is the substrate,
and what the defenses built against it actually depend on is the object of study.

\subsection{Passage-Filtering Defenses}
A broad class of defenses acts between retrieval and generation: inspect the
retrieved set, discard passages that look adversarial, and pass the rest to an
unmodified generator. PoisonedRAG evaluates four defenses in this spirit ---
paraphrasing, perplexity-based detection, duplicate-text filtering, and knowledge
expansion, the last of which enlarges the retrieved set rather than pruning
it~\cite{zou2025poisonedrag}. Three
families studied here belong to this class: RAGDefender, which reads cross-passage
embedding similarity structure within the retrieved
set~\cite{kim2025ragdefender}; FilterRAG, which scores each passage independently
by a lexical and semantic density statistic; and its learned ML-FilterRAG variant,
which replaces that fixed statistic with a classifier over density and fluency
features~\cite{edemacu2025filterrag}. Section~\ref{sec:threat} gives the
mechanisms. What matters here is the shape of the commitment: each filter reduces
detection to one measurable signal assumed to separate poisoned from benign
passages --- clustering or pairwise similarity structure, lexical and semantic
overlap with the query and candidate answer, or separability in a learned feature
space --- and a set-level signal is relative, so it needs benign passages to be
present in the retrieved set, whereas a per-passage signal does not.

\subsection{Generation-Time and Aggregation-Based Defenses}
A second class acts at generation rather than at passage admission.
Isolate-then-aggregate defenses query the generator separately on each retrieved
passage and aggregate the isolated responses, so that a bounded number of
corrupted passages can influence only a bounded number of them; RobustRAG builds
certifiable robustness on this structure~\cite{xiang2024robustrag}. The mechanism
differs in kind from filtering: no passage need be identified as adversarial or
removed, and the decision moves from which evidence is admitted to how
independently derived answers are combined. This gives an orthogonal defense
profile rather than a uniformly stronger one, since aggregation can hold where a
filter fails, can fail when the isolated responses agree on the poisoned answer,
and can abstain when the vote is contested, trading answer coverage for safety.
Our RobustRAG-KW implementation is inspired by RobustRAG-style
isolate-then-aggregate logic but is only a keyword/vote \emph{proxy}; it is not a
certified reproduction of RobustRAG, and no certification result is claimed for
it anywhere in this paper.

\subsection{Robustness Evaluation and Stress Testing}
Defenses of both kinds are usually reported as benchmark averages, against the
attack distribution they were designed for, at an operating point their authors
chose. Such a number does not identify which assumption a decision rule
operatively depends on, and a stated assumption need not be the operative one.
Defense-assumption stress testing instead constructs an operator against each
named assumption and measures whether the rule survives it. Oracle perturbations
act in the space the rule reads, where distribution-matching tools such as CORAL
and MMD give well-defined ways to move one group of representations toward
another~\cite{sun2016coral,gretton2012mmd} and where the clustering-robustness
literature reports that cluster assignments can be altered by small targeted
perturbations that are not themselves outliers, albeit outside a retrieval
setting~\cite{chhabra2020clustering,cina2022clustering};
text-space operators must instead yield real passages, for which the
semantics-preserving attack literature supplies the constraint vocabulary of
semantic preservation, grammaticality, masked-LM fluency, and sentence-encoder
cosine bounds~\cite{jin2020textfooler,li2020bertattack,morris2020textattack}.
Evidence does not transfer between them, so we separate four regimes
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:regimes}): oracle embedding and feature perturbation, testing
which assumption a rule depends on; fixed retrieved context with rewritten poison,
testing whether that assumption is reachable by real text; full-retrieval
replacement, testing whether the rewrite still retrieves; and answer-generation
attack success, testing whether the target answer is produced. A result in one
regime licenses no conclusion in the next.

\subsection{Positioning of This Work}
This paper is a defense-assumption stress test, not simply a new attack paper. We
do not claim a stronger attack than PoisonedRAG; we claim a way to find out what
a defense actually depends on, and we apply it to four defense families. What
prior evaluations do not separate is oracle fragility, text-realizable evasion,
retrieval-preserving evasion, and answer-level outcome on the same defenses and
the same queries, and without that separation a failure cannot be attributed to
the assumption that produced it. The contribution is a characterization of defense
failure modes and the design lessons that follow, not a universal attack and not a
new certified defense.

\section{Threat Model and Defenses Under Study}
\label{sec:threat}
% Budget: ~1.25 pg, contains Table I and Fig. 2.

\subsection{Threat Model}
\label{subsec:threatmodel}

\textit{System.} The system under study is a RAG pipeline: a dense retriever
returns the top-$k$ passages of a corpus for a given query, and a generator
answers that query conditioned on the retrieved passages under a fixed prompt
template. Everything the defense may inspect is derived from the query and from
that retrieved set.

\textit{Attacker capabilities and goal.} The attacker inserts or replaces a
small number of passages in the retrieval corpus and authors their text
directly. It is black-box with respect to the generator and controls neither
retrieval, nor the defense implementation, nor the defense's thresholds. Its
goal is targeted rather than degradational: for a chosen target query it fixes
one specific incorrect answer, which it knows at authoring time along with the
query, and it succeeds only when the generator produces that answer. A poisoned
passage must therefore meet both of the PoisonedRAG conditions --- be retrieved
for the target query, and support the target answer strongly enough to be
adopted~\cite{zou2025poisonedrag}.

\textit{Defender capabilities.} The defender sees the query and the retrieved
passages and has no poison labels at inference time. It acts either
post-retrieval, deciding which retrieved passages reach the generator
(RAGDefender, FilterRAG, ML-FilterRAG), or at generation time, controlling how
an answer is formed from those passages (RobustRAG-KW). Which of the two applies
is a property of the defense family and not something the attacker influences.

\textit{Poison budget and retrieval setting.} We author $N=\Npoison$ poisoned
passages per target query, the PoisonedRAG default, and use retrieval depth
$k=\Ktop$ as the setting for the stress-testing regimes.
Sec.~\ref{subsec:budget} gives the rationale for both values, including why the
poison-saturated $k=N$ regime is reported as a nominal condition rather than
used as the stress-testing setting.

\textit{Fixed context and full-retrieval replacement.} Mutations are evaluated
in two regimes, defined in Sec.~\ref{subsec:regimes}. Under fixed context the
retrieved top-$k$ membership and order are held constant by construction and
only the poisoned passages' text changes. Under full-retrieval replacement the
mutated passages \emph{replace} the original poisoned passages in the corpus and
retrieval is rerun, so original and mutated poison are never both present in a
context; duplicating the poison would inflate every downstream count and would
make retrieval a different experiment from the one we intend to run. We preserve
the self-query poison budget at five passages per target. When the shared
adversarial pool retrieves poison associated with another query, we count it
separately as cross-query poison rather than treating it as part of the target
attack budget.

\textit{Scope.} The object of study is defense robustness under stress testing,
not attack strength. We do not claim a universal or stronger attack: our
mutations are stress-test operators designed to probe defense assumptions, so a
result licenses a statement about the assumption it targets and the regime it
was measured in, and nothing broader.

\subsection{Defenses Under Study}
\label{subsec:families}

We study four defenses spanning the two layers of Sec.~\ref{sec:background}.
Each is described here only as far as its decision rule and the assumption that
rule encodes; implementations and operating points are given in
Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}.

\subsubsection{RAGDefender}
RAGDefender is a post-retrieval filter that reads pairwise structure among the
embeddings of the retrieved passages~\cite{kim2025ragdefender}. A first stage
estimates how many retrieved passages are adversarial, $\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{adv}}$,
from per-passage mean and median pairwise cosine similarity within the retrieved
set. A second stage accumulates, for each passage, how often it appears among
the $\binom{\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{adv}}}{2}$ most similar pairs, and removes the
$\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{adv}}$ passages with the highest resulting pair-frequency
score. The operative assumption is that poisoned passages reveal themselves through
concentrated overlap with the question and the candidate answer terms used by
the filter, relative to their own length. Under the evaluated configuration, 
our stress tests probe whether pair-frequency structure remains discriminative 
when poisoned passages are moved toward cleaner neighborhood structure. 
Our oracle analysis is scoped to the RAGDefender configuration evaluated in our experiments; 
we do not claim that every RAGDefender variant fails for the same mechanism without rerunning the
corresponding configuration.

\subsubsection{FilterRAG}
FilterRAG is a post-retrieval filter that scores each passage independently by a
semantic frequency-density statistic~\cite{edemacu2025filterrag}. A small
language model produces a candidate answer for the query; the query and answer
terms are matched into the passage, and the summed frequency of matched terms is
normalized by the passage's unique-word count. A passage whose density exceeds a
threshold $\epsilon$ is removed. The operative assumption is that poisoned
passages reveal themselves through concentrated overlap with the question and
the target answer relative to their own length. Unlike RAGDefender the statistic
is per-passage, because that signal is relative rather than per-passage, it is most meaningful
when the retrieved set contains benign passages against which poisoned structure
can be contrasted. We use the semantic matching mode as the paper-faithful FilterRAG setting and treat
exact matching as an ablation.

\subsubsection{ML-FilterRAG}
ML-FilterRAG replaces that fixed threshold with a learned passage classifier
over four features~\cite{edemacu2025filterrag}: the FilterRAG density score, the
matched-keyword frequency sum, passage perplexity, and the log-probability the
small model assigns to its own answer. A retrieved passage is removed before
generation when the classifier's poison probability reaches $t$. The operative
assumption is feature separability --- that poisoned and clean passages occupy
distinguishable regions of that feature space --- and because density is one of
the four features, a rewrite that lowers FilterRAG's statistic is expected to
transfer here. Our implementation is an \emph{ML-FilterRAG-top-$k$ proxy}: it
scores the retrieved top-$k$ set rather than reproducing the original
top-$s$, filter, then top-$k$ pipeline, and it is not a complete reproduction of
every setting of the published system.

\subsubsection{RobustRAG-KW}
RobustRAG-KW acts at generation time rather than at passage admission. It
isolates each retrieved passage into its own generator call, extracts and
normalizes a short answer from each, aggregates the isolated answers by keyword
vote, and abstains when the vote is contested. The mechanism differs in kind
from filtering because no passage is identified as adversarial or removed, which
gives it a different exposure profile: a rewrite that lowers a density statistic
may also weaken an isolated passage's persuasive power, so aggregation can hold
on cases where a filter is evaded, while a context in which most isolated
passages independently support the attacker's answer produces poison consensus
and defeats the vote, and a contested vote yields abstention rather than an
answer. The operative assumption is that clean evidence can contest the poison
vote. RobustRAG-KW is a RobustRAG-inspired keyword/vote proxy used to study the
generation-time aggregation paradigm; it is not a certified reproduction of
RobustRAG~\cite{xiang2024robustrag}. It supports a claim about \emph{where} in
the pipeline a defense acts --- an orthogonal generation-time defense profile ---
not a superiority claim over the passage filters.

\subsection{Defense Assumptions}
\label{subsec:assumptions}
Table~\ref{tab:defense_assumptions} states, for each family, the layer it acts

Fig.~\ref{fig:defense_assumption_map} gives the same relation as a conceptual

text-realizable by themselves: no perturbed vector has been shown to correspond
to a real passage. And each pressure is assumption-targeted by construction, so
a defense left unmoved by its own pressure is evidence about what that operator
reaches, not evidence of robustness in general.

\section{Stress-Testing Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
% Budget: ~1.10 pg, contains Fig. 1 and Table II.

\subsection{Overview and Evaluation Regimes}
\label{subsec:pipeline}
\label{subsec:regimes}
Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the pipeline: poison authoring, retrieval, defense,
generation, and metrics. Each experiment starts from a target query and the
$\Npoison$ PoisonedRAG passages authored for it; a \emph{stress-test operator}
then modifies either those passages' text or
the embeddings and features a defense computes from them, and an unmodified
defense implementation scores the result. Each operator enters at exactly one
stage, and the entry point determines what its result can license. We record
whether poisoned passages are removed before generation, survive retrieval, or
reach the generator and drive the answer. Retrieved poison is attributed by
origin: \emph{self-query poison} was authored for the query under test, while
\emph{cross-query poison} was authored for a different query of the shared
adversarial pool and never counts against the target's budget. Text-space
conditions are \emph{replacement-only mutations}, so the budget stays at
$N=\Npoison$ throughout. The mutation work is split into a \emph{development
set}, on which the rewrite prompts were designed, and a held-out
\emph{confirmation set} (Sec.~\ref{subsec:confirmation}); operating points,
models, and metric definitions are deferred to Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}.

Table~\ref{tab:evaluation_levels} defines the four regimes in which those
operators are evaluated. \emph{Oracle embedding and feature stress tests} modify
poison representations directly in the space a decision rule reads, with
retrieval membership and passage text held fixed; they establish which
statistical assumption the rule operatively depends on, and establish no attack.
\emph{Fixed-context text mutation} rewrites poison text into a retrieved context
whose membership and order are fixed by construction; it establishes that the
assumption is reachable by a real passage once poison is already retrieved, and
nothing about retrieval. \emph{Full-retrieval replacement} substitutes mutated
for original poison in the corpus and reruns retrieval under a preserved budget,
establishing that the rewrite still satisfies the retrieval condition.
\emph{Answer-generation evaluation} runs generation on the post-defense context
and scores the target incorrect answer, establishing an answer-level outcome for
the cases evaluated and only for those.

The regimes are ordered by realism but not by value: oracle tests expose boundary
conditions, fixed-context tests isolate defense sensitivity, full-retrieval
replacement tests retrievability, and answer-generation tests downstream impact.
They answer different questions and are reported separately; collapsing them into
one metric would discard the distinction the framework exists to draw.

\subsection{Oracle Perturbations}
\label{subsec:oracle}
Oracle perturbations are not passage rewrites. They are diagnostic interventions,
acting on a frozen space, used to identify which geometric or feature signals a
defense depends on.

\textit{E1 clean-anchor interpolation.} Each poisoned embedding is interpolated
toward an assigned clean anchor,
$\mathbf{z}' = \alpha \mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{poison}} + (1-\alpha)\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{anchor}}$,
under four anchor-assignment strategies --- rank-aligned, nearest-bijection,
farthest-bijection, and random --- and a sweep of $\alpha$ from $1.0$ to $0.3$.

\textit{Distribution matching.} MMD minimization performs kernel mean matching of
the poisoned group toward the clean group~\cite{gretton2012mmd} under a
preservation penalty bounding how far embeddings may move; CORAL-PCA and
CORAL-ridge align covariances with poisoned embeddings as source and clean
embeddings as target~\cite{sun2016coral}, in a reduced-rank and a regularized
form respectively.

\textit{Feature-space oracle.} The ML-FilterRAG proxy's four features are
interpolated toward clean targets, from a nearest-clean bijection to the global
clean centroid, putting the same locality question to a learned rule.

These are not text-space attacks and not learned domain-adaptation models; they
are formal embedding-space stress tests of a similarity-based decision rule.
% This sentence is deliberately retained near-verbatim from the notes file: it is
% the interpretation rule the rest of the paper leans on.
For RAGDefender, the oracle analysis is scoped to the evaluated configuration;
paper-faithful reruns may re-attribute the dominant failure mechanism.

\subsection{Targeted Text Mutation Families}
\label{subsec:families_mut}
Three hand-authored GPT rewrite families, one per targeted assumption, each
rewriting all $\Npoison$ poisoned passages of a query while preserving the
attacker's target wrong answer. The mutation families are stress-test operators
designed to probe defense assumptions.

\textit{RAGDefender-targeted.} Diversify the discourse frame of each passage and
reduce poison-poison semantic similarity, so the poison set stops being the
densest high-similarity subgraph and the pairwise clustering signal weakens.

\textit{FilterRAG-targeted.} Reduce lexical and semantic overlap with the query
and candidate-answer terms while preserving support for the same target wrong
answer, and raise unique-word count, so the frequency-density statistic falls.

\textit{ML-FilterRAG-targeted.} Make the passages read as ordinary clean
reference text, using clean retrieved passages as stylistic references, so the
learned feature profile shifts toward the clean distribution.

Rewrites are authored through fixed GPT prompt packets, one per family, so prompt
design is itself a development-set artifact. The current $\NdevQ$-query mutation
bundle is a development set used for prompt design and failure-mode discovery; a
larger held-out confirmation set is planned before final claims
(Sec.~\ref{subsec:confirmation}).

\subsection{Fixed-Context and Full-Retrieval Evaluation}
\label{subsec:fixedctx}
\label{subsec:fullret}
Under fixed context the retrieved top-$\Ktop$ membership and order are
reconstructed verbatim from the pilot artifacts and only the $\Npoison$ poisoned
passages' text differs between conditions, clean retrieved passages being
byte-identical across every condition of a case. Holding the retriever out
isolates a defense's sensitivity to passage content from any change in what
retrieval returns. We report removed poison, removed clean, the residual poison
fraction, and the defense-specific diagnostic score each rule computes
(Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}). Fixed-context success does not imply a practical corpus
attack because retrieval is bypassed.

Full-retrieval replacement closes that gap: mutated passages \emph{replace} the
original poisoned documents in the adversarial corpus and retrieval is rerun at
top-$k=\Ktop$, so the regime tests the PoisonedRAG retrieval
condition~\cite{zou2025poisonedrag} instead of assuming it. Full-retrieval
replacement preserves the self-query poison budget: original and mutated poisoned
passages are never included together for the same target query. Per case we track self-query poison retrieved, cross-query poison
retrieved, clean passages retrieved, and each defense's removal outcome.

\subsection{Answer Generation and RobustRAG-KW Evaluation}
\label{subsec:answergen}
Answer generation is run on shortlisted cases with no defense and after each
passage-filtering defense, so a defense's effect on the produced answer is
observed rather than inferred from removal counts. Strict ASR is a token-boundary
match of the attacker's target wrong answer in the generated text, and
abstentions are detected by the same detector RobustRAG-KW uses, so a refusal and
a correct answer are never merged into one outcome. Strict matching over-counts
on yes/no and contrastive answers, a limitation stated with the reporting
conventions of Sec.~\ref{sec:setup} and revisited in Sec.~\ref{sec:limitations}.

RobustRAG-KW runs in the same regime but reads a different signal: it isolates
each retrieved passage into its own generator call, extracts and normalizes a
short answer from each, aggregates the isolated answers by keyword vote, and
abstains when the vote is contested~\cite{xiang2024robustrag}. RobustRAG-KW is
evaluated as a generation-time aggregation proxy; it is not treated as a passage
filter and is not compared as a certified RobustRAG implementation, so it
contributes a mechanistically distinct generation-time profile rather than a
stronger one. Because each case costs one generator call per retrieved passage
plus an aggregation step, it is evaluated only on \emph{shortlisted} cases and
its outcomes are reported as counts, never rates. \NOOVERCLAIM{shortlist only}

\subsection{Reproducibility Controls}
\label{subsec:repro}
Retrieval and defense scoring are cached, so a rerun reproduces prior numbers
exactly rather than approximately, and answer generation is cached by
(model, prompt) hash so that RobustRAG-KW aggregation and the reports derived
from it can be regenerated from cached answers without new generator calls.
GPT prompt packets are hashed and committed per mutation family; confirmation-set
query identifiers are fixed in advance and held out; the poison budget is
replacement-only, preserved at $N=\Npoison$ in every mutated condition with no
original-plus-mutated duplication; and self-query versus cross-query poison is
accounted separately, so a case that retrieves poison authored for a different
pool query is never counted as if all retrieved poison targeted the case at hand.
Every evaluation script consumes committed artifacts and loads models read-only,
and the test suite makes no API calls.

\subsection{Poisoning Budget and Top-$k$}
\label{subsec:budget}
% Carried forward near-verbatim from the notes file: the strongest existing
% paragraph. Unicode quotes replaced with ASCII; citations to be added.
We set $N=\Npoison$ poisoned passages per target query to match the PoisonedRAG
default setting~\cite{zou2025poisonedrag}. We evaluate $k=5$ and $k=\Ktop$ to
separate two retrieval regimes. The $k=5$ setting corresponds to the PoisonedRAG
default and represents a poison-saturated context in which the number of
retrieved passages equals the number of injected poisoned passages. The
$k=\Ktop$ setting probes a mixed clean/poison regime where $k > N$, which
PoisonedRAG's own ablation identifies as the point where clean retrieved passages
enter the context while poisoned passages are still usually retrieved. This mixed
regime is particularly important for post-retrieval defenses such as RAGDefender
and FilterRAG, because their filtering decisions depend on the composition of the
retrieved top-$k$ set. The $k=5$ regime also violates RAGDefender's stated
requirement that at least one benign passage be retrieved, which is why
$k=\Ktop$ is the regime used for all stress-testing regimes above the nominal
one.

\subsection{Metrics}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textit{Removed poison} and \textit{removed clean}: counts of retrieved
        poisoned and clean passages the defense removes.
  \item \textit{Residual poison fraction}: the poison share of the \emph{surviving}
        context, not of the retrieved set. This definition differs from the
        intuitive reading and must be stated explicitly in the paper.
  \item \textit{Strict ASR}: the attacker's target answer appears in the generated
        answer under token-boundary matching.
  \item \textit{Abstention}: the generator or aggregator declines to answer;
        counted separately from a correct answer.
  \item \textit{Wrong-answer vote share} and \textit{correct-answer vote share}:
        for RobustRAG-KW, the fraction of isolated per-passage answers voting for
        the attacker's answer and for the gold answer.
  \item \textit{Self-query versus cross-query poison}: retrieved poisoned passages
        authored for this query versus for a different pool query, reported
        separately.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{RQ3: Do targeted text rewrites weaken passage filters?}

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}
% Budget: ~0.40 pg. Compress to a single dense paragraph per group if needed.
% Every item here is already documented in the repository and must be stated
% rather than left for a reviewer to find.

\textit{Development-set size.} The targeted mutation work covers $\NdevQ$
queries and $\NdevBundles$ bundles, with one rewrite per poison slot per family,
so per-family statistics are means over queries and not over repeated independent
attempts.

\textit{HotpotQA-only current evidence.} All stress-testing regimes use one
dataset and one retriever; cross-dataset and cross-retriever generality is not
measured.

visibly on yes/no answers, and a strict hit co-occurring with the correct answer
requires adjudication.

RobustRAG; no certification result is claimed.

outcome distribution is not that of the benchmark.
